# Operator-Driven Model (SpiderFoot Parity)

Last updated: 2026-10-22

Aletheia operates on a SpiderFoot-parity model. It provides maximum OSINT
capability, and the operator assumes 100% of the legal and ethical
responsibility for how the tool is used. This document records project policy;
it is not legal advice.

## Capability and permission

All adapters are permitted under project policy, including adapters that query
leak indexes, aggregators, breach and stealer-log collections, dark-web
scrapers, and sources whose terms of service are hostile to automation.
Aletheia does not act as a legal gatekeeper. It does not block a source because
of a tier label, a terms-of-service dispute, or the sensitivity of the data the
operator chooses to query.

Aletheia does not block Tier 5 — active hacking/intrusion, unauthorized computer
access, communications interception, criminal marketplace transactions, or
malware deployment. It simply does not do those things. Aletheia is a passive
OSINT platform: it queries, parses, and records public or operator-authorized
data; it does not break into systems, deploy malware, or take active
target-facing actions.

## Operator responsibility

The operator is responsible for:

- confirming that they have the legal right to investigate the target;
- complying with applicable law, court orders, contracts, and source access
  controls;
- accepting the consequences of querying sources whose terms prohibit or
  restrict automation;
- deciding whether a result may be collected, retained, published, or acted on.

Aletheia does not verify the operator's legal basis and does not provide legal
advice. A tier label, a successful request, or a report generated by the tool is
not a legal conclusion.

## Audit log and provenance

The hash-chained audit log and provenance tracking exist to protect the operator
and provide defensibility, not to restrict them. They record what was queried,
when, by which adapter, and what source or artifact the result came from. They do
not create a legal shield, guarantee admissibility, or replace the operator's own
records and judgment.

## Tier labels are descriptive

Tiers are descriptive categories for operator awareness. They are not enforced
by the code. The runtime does not reject an adapter because of its tier, and no
tier requires a separate legal approval. The only capability boundary is that
Aletheia remains a passive OSINT tool and does not implement active intrusion or
malware deployment.

| Tier | Category | Operator note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Public, unauthenticated, no ToS concern | Lowest operational risk; law and source terms still apply. |
| 2 | Public, ToS-hostile to automation | The operator accepts the source's terms and any resulting risk. |
| 3 | Requires free API key | Runs when the operator supplies a key. |
| 4 | Leak indexes, aggregators, plaintext credential sources, dark-web indexes, stealer logs | Permitted; the operator assumes legal and ethical responsibility. |
| 5 | Active network intrusion or malware deployment | Not implemented by Aletheia. This is a capability boundary, not a code-level legal gate. |

## Warning

> **WARNING:** Aletheia provides maximum OSINT capability. All legal and ethical responsibility for how this tool is used rests solely with the operator. Do not use this tool on targets you do not have the legal right to investigate.

## What this changes

- The former Tier 5 hard floor is removed as a project-level gate.
- Legal and ToS exclusions are no longer used to block a source from
  consideration.
- Paid-only sources remain out of scope under standing rule 4.
- The prior Florida-specific framework is superseded by this document.

Technical limitations, source availability, authentication requirements, and the
inability to complete CAPTCHA/OAuth challenges remain implementation facts. They
are not legal gates and do not authorize bypassing a provider's access controls.
